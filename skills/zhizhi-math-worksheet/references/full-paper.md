# Full-paper variants

Use a complete photo/PDF as the reference for a new paper of similar scope, structure, and difficulty, with a separate answer key. The parent does not need to specify a question-type inventory.

Read the source and form a brief internal outline: sections, question counts, concepts, number ranges, essential diagram relationships, and approximate difficulty. Retain uncertainties; an unseen reverse side is not covered.

- Preserve the major sections and approximate length by default. Vary quantities, settings, diagrams, and options; solve the new questions again.
- Preserve the tested action. A task that requires counting objects in a picture should not become a text-only question that supplies those counts.
- Recheck arithmetic range, divisibility, solution uniqueness, and diagram constraints after changing numbers. Do not retain an old picture or answer that no longer fits.
- Same-structure variants suit parallel practice. Change representations, the requested quantity, or conditions when transfer is requested, without adding unlearned concepts.
- Reuse the layout style without copying names, school identifiers, handwriting, or grading marks.
- Follow the main skill's language policy. An English request about a Chinese paper does not by itself authorize translating the student paper or replacing its curriculum.

Compare the output with the source outline before delivery, including harder and diagram-based questions. Apply the main skill's solution and PDF-preview checks.
