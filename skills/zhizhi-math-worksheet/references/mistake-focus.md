# Optional mistake-focused practice

This is a strategy within `question_list`, not another input branch. Parents or the coach select the questions and may supply student work and a diagnosis. Design correct, solvable practice around the observed difficulty. Without error evidence, use the ordinary list workflow rather than requiring a diagnosis first.

## Evidence

Prefer explicitly selected or confirmed incorrect questions. When reading marked work, cross-check the problem, student's answer, and marks; a circle or blank is not sufficient evidence of an error.

For each target, retain a short record of the source ID, question, visible student answer, correct solution, concept, observed error, and whether its cause is confirmed. Request missing question details only when needed, not an entire learning archive.

Do not infer a cause from a wrong final answer alone. A multiplication error might involve grouping, recall, or copying. Label uncertain causes as hypotheses and use a small discriminating task when useful. Do not promote hypotheses into durable diagnoses.

## Practice design

- Group similar difficulties instead of mechanically expanding every source mistake into a full set.
- Begin with a new same-structure question to check whether the original difficulty remains.
- Use contrast or representation changes within the learned scope. Change the requested quantity or form when transfer is the goal.
- Respect requested length. Around ten minutes is a reasonable unspecified short practice; split oversized sets or state what is covered.
- Separate independent checks from guided tasks. If checking for omitted units, conditions, or minutes, avoid a first-item answer format that directly reminds the learner to supply the missing part. Later scaffolding is acceptable.

The private answer manifest should connect each new question to its source and record its concept, observed error, tentative cause/confidence, purpose, solution, acceptable alternatives, and relevant steps to observe. A single incorrect response does not establish its cause.

## Evaluation

Check whether each new question preserves the relevant difficulty, stays within scope, and avoids revealing the answer, then verify math and printed layout. Retain the source-to-question-to-purpose mapping for review. This checks design quality, not mastery or instructional effectiveness.

After actual student work, the coach may assess independent performance and reasoning, with later spaced checks if needed. Generation itself must not change mastery status. A synthetic clock-reading example has been reviewed; real learner outcomes remain untested. See [Validation record](validation.md).
